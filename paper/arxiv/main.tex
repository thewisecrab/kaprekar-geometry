\begin{abstract}
The Kaprekar routine is a finite digit-rearrangement dynamical system whose
best-known decimal fixed point is 6174.  Outer-digit difference coordinates
for this routine are classical.  Starting from that prior art, this paper
gives an explicit integer linear reconstruction, proves that it is injective,
and derives the exact one-step image size
\[
  \left|\ImMap K_{b,n}\right|
  =\binom{b+\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1}{b-1}.
\]
The restriction of the full map to its one-step image is conjugate to a
reduced map on spectra.  With exact spectrum-fiber weights, this conjugacy
recovers the complete functional graph, raw-state basin sizes, hitting-time
histograms, and entropy evolution in polynomial time for every fixed base.
Certified decimal computations show that nontrivial cycles, rather than fixed
points, capture most raw inputs at widths five through eight.

The same adjacent-gap operator yields normalized coordinates for sorted
top-$k$ language-model logits.  We prove reconstruction, singularity at a
flat spectrum, near-flat sensitivity, change-of-$k$ behavior, sharp omitted-tail
bounds, and a distribution-free calibration impossibility.  These coordinates
are exactly an invertible reparameterization of relative sorted logits; no
predictive improvement follows from the reparameterization alone.  We also
derive the exact accept-and-residual condition for distribution-preserving
speculative sampling and show why a target-only rank or margin gate is not
exact in general.  An independent integer-arithmetic checker exhaustively
verifies 49 finite systems comprising 3,816,497 raw states, while a separate
Lean development mechanizes the core pairing identities and part of the
counting argument.  No empirical large-language-model performance claim is
made.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
Kaprekar routine; finite dynamical systems; functional graphs; exact basin
weights; entropy; sorted logits; uncertainty estimation; speculative decoding.

\section{Introduction}

Kaprekar's digit-rearrangement routine begins with a fixed-width digit string,
sorts its digits in descending and ascending order, and subtracts the latter
number from the former.  The decimal fixed points 495 and 6174 made the routine
widely known \cite{kaprekar1949,kaprekar1955}.  The broader mathematical object
is not a single constant, however, but a family of finite functional graphs
indexed by the base and the width.  Longer widths and other bases exhibit
multiple fixed points, genuine cycles, and nonuniform basins
\cite{hasse1978,prichett1981,walden2005,kay2022,kay2024}.

The first purpose of this paper is to make a precise compression theorem for
these graphs.  Prichett, Ludington, and Lapenta already observed that a
Kaprekar transform depends entirely on ordered outer-digit differences
\cite{prichett1981}; this dependence is therefore not claimed as new.  The
contribution developed here is the chain of consequences obtained from an
explicit linear map and its injectivity: an exact image count, a conjugacy
after the first step, reconstruction of the full functional graph from a
weighted reduced graph, polynomial fixed-base basin computation, and an exact
entropy pushforward identity.

The second purpose is to delimit a proposed analogy with language-model
logits.  Sorted top-$k$ logits can be represented by normalized adjacent gaps.
This is a clean coordinate system, but it is an invertible reparameterization
of relative sorted logits, not a source of additional information.  It also
discards token identity and, unless supplied separately, tail information.
Accordingly, this paper proves only structural identities and impossibility
results for these coordinates.  It does not report or imply a gain in
hallucination detection, selective prediction, routing, or decoding speed.

The main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item an explicit factorization $K_{b,n}=\Lambda_{b,n}\circ\Sigma_{b,n}$,
        a superincreasing proof of injectivity, and an exact image-cardinality
        theorem valid also for width one;
  \item an exact conjugacy on the one-step image and a reconstruction theorem
        for the entire raw functional graph using spectrum-fiber weights;
  \item exact raw basin, depth, and entropy formulas, together with certified
        decimal results showing cycle-dominated dynamics at widths five
        through eight;
  \item a corrected account of normalized sorted-logit gaps, including their
        exact information content, degeneracies, sensitivity, tail limits,
        and distribution-free limitations; and
  \item an exact acceptance/residual theorem for speculative sampling and a
        reproducibility package with independent exhaustive certificates and
        partial formal verification.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Evidence discipline.}
Universal algebraic results, finite computational certificates, and empirical
hypotheses are kept separate.  Exhaustive computation can prove a declared
finite instance and can falsify an implementation, but it is not substituted
for proofs over infinitely many $(b,n)$.  Conversely, theorems about a
coordinate system do not establish predictive value on real model outputs.

\section{Prior work and novelty boundary}

\subsection{Kaprekar dynamics}

The classical literature classified four-digit constants and decimal fixed
points \cite{hasse1978,prichett1981}.  The 1981 paper explicitly introduced
arbitrary-base outer-difference tuples and stated that the transformation
depends entirely on them.  Later work studied higher bases, symmetries, fixed
points, cycles, and convergence distances
\cite{walden2005,devlin2020,nuez2022,kay2022,kay2024}.  Iwasaki gave an
explicit modern classification of decimal nonzero fixed points and clarified
the distinction between a fixed point and a globally attracting Kaprekar
constant \cite{iwasaki2024}.  Dahl analyzed attractor-basin entropy and
coarse-grained drift fields for decimal systems \cite{dahl2026}.

Very recent work by Chen, Ono, Schwartz, and Thakur gives a much stronger
number-theoretic classification for four-digit odd bases: after a bounded
transient, the stable dynamics are conjugate to projective doubling
\cite{chenkaprekar2026}.  That specialized theorem and its separate Lean
artifact should not be replaced by generic enumeration.  The present results
are complementary: they hold for arbitrary base and width and expose exact
image, graph, and weight structure, but do not reproduce the odd-base
classification.

The defensible novelty claim is therefore deliberately narrow.  We do not
claim the discovery of outer-gap reduction.  We claim the explicit
superincreasing encoding, exact image cardinality, post-first-step conjugacy,
complete weighted-graph reconstruction, and their information-theoretic
consequences.

\subsection{Logit uncertainty and speculative decoding}

Output-side uncertainty estimates coexist with semantic and internal-state
methods \cite{azaria2023,farquhar2024,kossen2024,ma2025}.  Recent evidence
shows that entropy-only selective policies can fail in model-dependent ways
and that hybrid scores can improve measured risk--coverage behavior on some
benchmarks \cite{phillips2026}.  Other direct baselines include logit-magnitude
methods \cite{zhu2026} and local sorted-logit geometry such as Min-$k$
sampling \cite{ding2026}.  These works make an information-matched raw
relative-logit baseline mandatory for any future experiment on the coordinates
studied here.

Exact speculative decoding uses modified rejection sampling to preserve the
target distribution \cite{leviathan2023,chen2023speculative}.  Margin-aware
relaxed verification has already been studied at model scale by MARS
\cite{song2026mars}, and recent acceptance-certificate theory gives sharper
criteria for several deterministic and relaxed rules \cite{sharma2026}.
Consequently, the margin identity in this paper is presented as an elementary
diagnostic, not as a novel distribution-preserving decoder.

\section{Kaprekar maps and ordered outer gaps}

Fix a base $b\geq2$ and a width $n\geq1$.  Let
\[
  X_{b,n}=\{0,1,\ldots,b^n-1\},
\]
where every element is represented by exactly $n$ base-$b$ digits, including
leading zeros.  If the digits sorted in descending order are
\[
  a_1\geq a_2\geq\cdots\geq a_n,
\]
define the fixed-width Kaprekar map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kaprekar-map}
  K_{b,n}(x)
  =\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i b^{n-i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i b^{i-1}.
\end{equation}
Set $m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.

\begin{definition}[Ordered outer-gap spectrum]
For $1\leq j\leq m$, define
\[
  \Delta_j=a_j-a_{n+1-j}.
\]
The spectrum map is $\Sigma_{b,n}(x)=(\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_m)$, with
codomain
\[
  S_{b,n}=\{\Delta\in\Z^m:
  b-1\geq\Delta_1\geq\cdots\geq\Delta_m\geq0\}.
\]
For $n=1$, $S_{b,1}$ contains the unique empty spectrum.
\end{definition}

Every actual spectrum lies in $S_{b,n}$.  The coordinate bounds are immediate,
and for $j<m$,
\[
 \Delta_j-\Delta_{j+1}
 =(a_j-a_{j+1})+(a_{n-j}-a_{n+1-j})\geq0.
\]

\begin{definition}[Linear reconstruction]
For $1\leq j\leq m$, let
\[
  c_j=b^{n-j}-b^{j-1},
  \qquad
  \Lambda_{b,n}(\Delta)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_j\Delta_j.
\]
For $m=0$, the empty sum is zero.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Exact factorization]\label{thm:factorization}
For every $x\in X_{b,n}$,
\[
  K_{b,n}(x)=\Lambda_{b,n}(\Sigma_{b,n}(x)).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In \eqref{eq:kaprekar-map}, pair the term indexed by $j$ with the term indexed
by $n+1-j$.  Their combined contribution is
\[
  (a_j-a_{n+1-j})(b^{n-j}-b^{j-1})=c_j\Delta_j.
\]
If $n$ is odd, the unpaired middle coefficient is zero.  Summing the $m$
pairs proves the identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Spectrum surjectivity]\label{lem:surjectivity}
The map $\Sigma_{b,n}:X_{b,n}\to S_{b,n}$ is surjective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For any $\Delta\in S_{b,n}$, use the sorted length-$n$ digit string
\[
  (\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_m,0,\ldots,0).
\]
Its outer differences are exactly $\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_m$.  When $n=1$,
every raw digit has the unique empty spectrum.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Superincreasing coefficients]\label{lem:superincreasing}
For every $1\leq j\leq m$,
\[
  c_j>(b-1)\sum_{k=j+1}^{m}c_k.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Finite geometric summation gives
\begin{align*}
 c_j-(b-1)\sum_{k=j+1}^{m}c_k
 &=b^{n-m}+b^m-b^j-b^{j-1}.
\end{align*}
Because $j\leq m$ and $n-m\geq m$,
\[
 b^j+b^{j-1}
 \leq b^m+b^{m-1}
 <2b^m
 \leq b^{n-m}+b^m.
\]
The difference is therefore strictly positive.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Injectivity and exact image size]\label{thm:image}
The map $\Lambda_{b,n}$ is injective on $S_{b,n}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:image-cardinality}
  \left|\ImMap K_{b,n}\right|
  =|S_{b,n}|
  =\binom{m+b-1}{b-1}
  =\binom{b+\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For distinct $\Delta,\Delta'$, let $j$ be their first differing coordinate and
orient them so that $\Delta_j-\Delta'_j\geq1$.  Every later coordinate
difference has magnitude at most $b-1$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:superincreasing}
implies
\[
 \Lambda(\Delta)-\Lambda(\Delta')
 \geq c_j-(b-1)\sum_{k>j}c_k>0.
\]
Thus $\Lambda$ is injective.  Theorem~\ref{thm:factorization} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:surjectivity} give
$\ImMap K=\Lambda(S_{b,n})$.

To count spectra, let $q_r$ be the number of coordinates of $\Delta$ equal to
$r$, for $r=0,\ldots,b-1$.  The weakly decreasing spectrum is uniquely
determined by the weak composition
\[
 q_0+q_1+\cdots+q_{b-1}=m.
\]
Stars and bars gives $\binom{m+b-1}{b-1}$.  This argument also covers $m=0$,
where there is one empty spectrum.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-base compression]\label{cor:compression}
For fixed $b$,
\[
 |\ImMap K_{b,n}|
 =\frac{m^{b-1}}{(b-1)!}+O(m^{b-2})
 =\Theta(n^{b-1}),
\]
whereas $|X_{b,n}|=b^n$.  Hence the one-step image ratio tends to zero
exponentially in $n$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
The spectrum has tuple length $m$, but at fixed base it can be stored using the
$b$ histogram counts $q_0,\ldots,q_{b-1}$ with one sum constraint.  It is
therefore misleading to call $m$ an unqualified effective dimension.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact conjugacy and complete functional graphs}

Write $\Sigma=\Sigma_{b,n}$, $\Lambda=\Lambda_{b,n}$, $K=K_{b,n}$, and define
\[
  R=\Sigma\circ\Lambda:S_{b,n}\to S_{b,n},
  \qquad I=\ImMap K=\Lambda(S_{b,n}).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Post-first-step conjugacy]\label{thm:conjugacy}
The map $\Lambda:S_{b,n}\to I$ is a bijection satisfying
\[
  K|_I\circ\Lambda=\Lambda\circ R.
\]
Consequently $K|_I$ and $R$ have corresponding cycles with identical least
periods, and for every $x\in X_{b,n}$ and $t\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iterate}
  K^t(x)=\Lambda R^{t-1}\Sigma(x).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:image} makes $\Lambda$ a bijection onto $I$.  Direct
composition gives
\[
 K\Lambda=\Lambda\Sigma\Lambda=\Lambda R.
\]
This is the conjugacy identity, and \eqref{eq:iterate} follows by induction.
Bijective conjugacy preserves least periods.
\end{proof}

Semiconjugacy alone would not justify exact cycle correspondence; injectivity
is the missing step.  Every raw orbit enters $I$ after one application of $K$.

For a spectrum $\Delta$, define its raw fiber weight
\[
  w_{b,n}(\Delta)=|\Sigma^{-1}(\Delta)|.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Complete graph from a weighted reduced graph]
\label{thm:full-graph}
For every $\Delta\in S_{b,n}$,
\[
  \indeg_K(\Lambda\Delta)=w_{b,n}(\Delta).
\]
The entire functional graph of $K$ is recovered, up to the integer labels of
anonymous leaves, by relabeling each reduced vertex $\Delta$ as
$\Lambda\Delta$ and attaching exactly
\[
  w_{b,n}(\Delta)-\indeg_R(\Delta)
\]
additional indegree-zero source leaves to it.  Every state outside $I$ has
indegree zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Injectivity gives
\[
 K(x)=\Lambda\Delta
 \iff \Lambda\Sigma(x)=\Lambda\Delta
 \iff \Sigma(x)=\Delta.
\]
Thus the full indegree equals the fiber size.  A preimage already in $I$ has
the form $\Lambda\Delta'$ and maps to $\Lambda\Delta$ exactly when
$R(\Delta')=\Delta$.  These are the $\indeg_R(\Delta)$ reduced predecessors.
All other preimages lie outside $I$; by definition of $I=\ImMap K$, such a
state cannot itself have a predecessor.
\end{proof}

The theorem reconstructs graph structure and multiplicities.  Recovering the
original integer label of every source leaf still requires enumerating or
otherwise describing its spectrum fiber.

\section{Exact fiber weights, basins, and information loss}

Let $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ be a sorted digit multiset and let $c_d(a)$ count the
occurrences of digit $d$.  The number of fixed-width strings represented by
$a$ is
\[
  \operatorname{mult}(a)=\frac{n!}{\prod_{d=0}^{b-1}c_d(a)!}.
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fiber-weight}
 w_{b,n}(\Delta)
 =\sum_{\substack{b-1\geq a_1\geq\cdots\geq a_n\geq0\\
                   a_j-a_{n+1-j}=\Delta_j}}
   \frac{n!}{\prod_{d=0}^{b-1}c_d(a)!}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact raw basin weight]\label{thm:basin}
Let $C$ be a cycle of $R$, and let
\[
  \Basin_R(C)=\{\Delta:R^t(\Delta)\in C\text{ for some }t\geq0\}.
\]
Then $\Lambda(C)$ is the corresponding full cycle and
\[
  |\Basin_K(\Lambda C)|
  =\sum_{\Delta\in\Basin_R(C)}w_{b,n}(\Delta).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The spectrum fibers partition $X_{b,n}$.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:conjugacy}, a raw state reaches $\Lambda(C)$ exactly when its
spectrum reaches $C$.  Summing precisely those fiber sizes proves the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact hitting-time histogram]\label{cor:hitting}
If $\Delta\notin C$ has reduced distance $d_R(\Delta,C)$, every state in its
fiber has raw hitting time $d_R(\Delta,C)+1$.  If $\Delta\in C$, exactly one
state in its fiber is already on $\Lambda(C)$ and the remaining
$w_{b,n}(\Delta)-1$ states enter the cycle in one step.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a noncycle spectrum, \eqref{eq:iterate} and injectivity show that first
cycle entry occurs one step after its reduced entry.  For $\Delta\in C$, the
cyclic permutation $R|_C$ has a unique predecessor $\gamma\in C$.  The state
$\Lambda\gamma$ is the unique cycle member whose spectrum is $\Delta$; every
other state in that fiber maps to $\Lambda\Delta$ in one step.
\end{proof}

There are $\binom{b+n-1}{b-1}$ sorted digit multisets.  For fixed $b$, this
number and the reduced-state count are both $\Theta(n^{b-1})$.  Each transition,
multinomial weight, and graph traversal uses arithmetic on integers with bit
length polynomial in $n$.  Equations \eqref{eq:fiber-weight} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:basin} therefore give a polynomial-time exact algorithm in the
standard bit model for every fixed base, without enumerating all $b^n$ strings.

\begin{theorem}[Entropy pushforward]\label{thm:entropy}
Let $X$ have any distribution $\mu$ on $X_{b,n}$ and let
$\nu=\Sigma_*\mu$.  For every $t\geq1$,
\[
 (K^t)_*\mu=\Lambda_*(R^{t-1})_*\nu,
 \qquad
 H(K^t(X))=H(R^{t-1}(\Sigma X)).
\]
Under a uniform raw input,
\[
 \Pr(\Sigma X=\Delta)=\frac{w_{b,n}(\Delta)}{b^n}
\]
and the exact one-step Shannon information loss is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-loss}
 H(X\mid K(X))
 =\sum_{\Delta\in S_{b,n}}
   \frac{w_{b,n}(\Delta)}{b^n}\log_2 w_{b,n}(\Delta).
\end{equation}
It obeys
\[
 H(X\mid K(X))
 \geq n\log_2b-\log_2\binom{m+b-1}{b-1}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The pushforward identity is the random-variable form of
\eqref{eq:iterate}.  The injective relabeling $\Lambda$ preserves atom
probabilities and hence discrete entropy.  Under the uniform law, conditioning
on $K(X)=\Lambda\Delta$ leaves a uniform choice among exactly
$w_{b,n}(\Delta)$ raw states, proving \eqref{eq:entropy-loss}.  Finally,
$K$ is deterministic, so $H(X\mid K(X))=n\log_2b-H(K(X))$, and entropy on a
support of size $|\ImMap K|$ is at most the logarithm of that size.
\end{proof}

For fixed $b$, the support-based loss grows as
\[
 n\log_2b-(b-1)\log_2n+O(1).
\]
This is a concrete negative engineering consequence.  The map has a
polynomial-size image and efficiently constructible collision fibers; it must
not be represented as a cryptographic hash, entropy source, or reversible
encoding.

\section{Normalized adjacent gaps for sorted logits}

Let a vocabulary logit vector have size $V$, select its top $k\geq2$ order
statistics, and write
\[
  s_1\geq s_2\geq\cdots\geq s_k.
\]
Assume first that $D=s_1-s_k>0$ and define normalized adjacent gaps
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logit-gaps}
  u_j=\frac{s_j-s_{j+1}}{D},
  \qquad j=1,\ldots,k-1.
\end{equation}
These coordinates were called the Kaprekar gap simplex in the original
proposal.  Mathematically, they are normalized spacings of sorted logits.

\begin{theorem}[Exact information content]\label{thm:logit-reconstruction}
The vector $u$ lies in
\[
  \left\{u\in\R_{\geq0}^{k-1}:\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}u_j=1\right\},
\]
a simplex of affine dimension $k-2$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logit-reconstruction}
  s_i-s_k=D\sum_{j=i}^{k-1}u_j.
\end{equation}
Consequently, $u$ is a bijective coordinate for sorted top-$k$ spectra modulo
positive scaling and translation; $(D,u)$ is a bijective coordinate modulo
translation and exactly determines the top-$k$-conditional softmax law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Nonnegativity follows from sorting.  Telescoping gives
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{k-1}u_j
 =\frac{s_1-s_k}{D}=1,
 \qquad
 D\sum_{j=i}^{k-1}u_j=s_i-s_k.
\]
The reconstruction formula proves the stated bijections.  Softmax restricted
and renormalized to the top $k$ depends only on relative logits.
\end{proof}

The exact connection with the digit construction is the shared interval-sum
operator.  For the applicable ranges of $j$,
\[
 a_j-a_{n+1-j}=\sum_{i=j}^{n-j}(a_i-a_{i+1}),
 \qquad
 s_j-s_{k+1-j}=D\sum_{i=j}^{k-j}u_i.
\]
No stronger transfer theorem between the two application domains is claimed.

Define the translation-quotiented vector
\[
  r(s)=(s_1-s_k,\ldots,s_{k-1}-s_k,0).
\]

\begin{corollary}[No information creation]\label{cor:no-information}
The observations $r(s)$ and $(D,u)$ are bijectively related and therefore have
identical Bayes-optimal risk for every target and loss.  Since $(D,u)$ is a
deterministic function of the unrestricted sorted vector $s$, the latter can
have lower Bayes risk.  Equality for every decision problem is guaranteed, for
example, by $Y\perp s_k\mid r(s)$.
\end{corollary}

A varying common offset can predict $Y$ while leaving $(D,u)$ unchanged.  An
information-matched benchmark must therefore compare a same-capacity model on
relative sorted logits.  Any gain over that comparator can arise only from
finite-sample inductive bias, regularization, numerical conditioning, or a
capacity constraint, not from additional information.

\subsection{Degeneracy, sensitivity, and the top-k choice}

\begin{proposition}[Flat-spectrum singularity]
For $k\geq3$, the map in \eqref{eq:logit-gaps} has no continuous extension to
$D=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose distinct simplex points $u\neq v$.  Reconstruct two sorted spectra with
bottom logit zero and spread $\varepsilon$.  Both approach the same all-zero
vector as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, while their normalized coordinates remain
$u$ and $v$.  A continuous extension would assign two limits to the same
point.
\end{proof}

For $k=2$, $u=(1)$ whenever $D>0$, so it contains no shape information.
Implementations should expose a flat-spectrum flag rather than silently divide
by an epsilon and report an ordinary simplex point.

\begin{proposition}[Near-flat sensitivity]\label{prop:sensitivity}
Let sorted spectra $s,t$ have spreads $D_s,D_t\geq d>0$ and satisfy
$\|s-t\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon$.  Then
\[
  \|u(s)-u(t)\|_\infty\leq\frac{4\varepsilon}{d}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each adjacent gap changes by at most $2\varepsilon$, and the spread changes by
at most $2\varepsilon$.  For corresponding gaps $0\leq g'\leq D_t$,
\[
 \left|\frac{g}{D_s}-\frac{g'}{D_t}\right|
 \leq\frac{|g-g'|}{D_s}
 +\frac{g'|D_t-D_s|}{D_sD_t}
 \leq\frac{4\varepsilon}{d}.
\]
\end{proof}

The $1/d$ factor captures unavoidable ill-conditioning near the flat stratum.
Top-$k$ token membership has an additional discontinuity at cutoff ties.

\begin{proposition}[Exact change of $k$]
If a nondegenerate top-$k$ spectrum is extended by $s_{k+1}\leq s_k$ and
$D'=s_1-s_{k+1}$, then
\[
  u'_j=\frac{D}{D'}u_j\quad(1\leq j<k),
  \qquad
  u'_k=\frac{s_k-s_{k+1}}{D'}.
\]
\end{proposition}

Thus changing $k$ rescales every existing coordinate.  Any experiment must
predeclare $k$, sweep it, or use an explicitly multiscale representation.

\subsection{Sharp omitted-tail bounds}

Set
\[
 r_i=s_i-s_k=D\sum_{j=i}^{k-1}u_j,
 \qquad
 A=\sum_{i=1}^{k}e^{r_i}.
\]
For omitted logits $z_\ell\leq s_k$, define their partition mass relative to
$e^{s_k}$ by
\[
  T=\sum_{\ell>k}e^{z_\ell-s_k}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Tail bounds]\label{thm:tail-bounds}
For vocabulary size $V\geq k$,
\[
 0\leq T\leq V-k,
 \qquad
 M_k=\frac{A}{A+T},
 \qquad
 p_1=\frac{e^D}{A+T},
\]
where $M_k$ is the full-softmax top-$k$ mass and $p_1$ is the full-softmax
top-token probability.  Therefore
\[
 \frac{A}{A+V-k}\leq M_k\leq1,
 \qquad
 \frac{e^D}{A+V-k}\leq p_1\leq\frac{e^D}{A}.
\]
The lower bounds are attained when every omitted logit equals $s_k$.  If
$V=k$, then $T=0$ and $(D,u)$ determines the complete softmax distribution.
If $V>k$ and all logits are finite, the upper bounds are strict but sharp
suprema as omitted logits tend to minus infinity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each of the $V-k$ tail summands is at most one and nonnegative.  The formulas
follow by dividing the full partition by $e^{s_k}$.  Both $M_k$ and $p_1$ are
decreasing functions of $T$, proving the bounds and equality conditions.
\end{proof}

When $V>k$, identical $(D,u)$ values can have different tail mass, full
entropy, and unconditional top-token probability.  For example, give one tail
token relative weight $t\in(0,1]$.  If $P$ is the top-$k$-conditional law and
$q=t/(A+t)$, the resulting entropy approaches
\[
  h_2(q)+(1-q)H(P),
\]
which is nonconstant in $q$.  Recording the additional statistic
\[
 \tau=\operatorname{logsumexp}(z_{\mathrm{tail}})-s_k
\]
determines $T=e^\tau$ and all top-$k$ full-softmax probabilities.  For an empty
tail, the consistent convention is $\tau=-\infty$.

\begin{corollary}[Exact rank ratio]\label{cor:rank-ratio}
For $1\leq r\leq k$,
\[
  \frac{p_r}{p_1}
  =\exp\left(-D\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}u_j\right).
\]
\end{corollary}

This identity is local to one target distribution.  It is neither a semantic
correctness guarantee nor a speculative-acceptance certificate.

\subsection{Distribution-free correctness is impossible}

\begin{proposition}[No universal logit-only calibrator]\label{prop:no-free-lunch}
Fix any measurable proposed correctness probability
$q:\R^V\to[0,1]$.  There exist two joint laws with the same deterministic
logit vector $Z=z_0$, but with correctness $Y=1$ almost surely under one law
and $Y=0$ almost surely under the other.  Consequently
\[
 \max\{\operatorname{Brier}_P(q),\operatorname{Brier}_Q(q)\}
 =\max\{(1-q(z_0))^2,q(z_0)^2\}\geq\frac14.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The observation, and hence $q(Z)$, is identical in both laws, while their
conditional correctness probabilities are zero and one.  Either
$q(z_0)\geq1/2$ or $1-q(z_0)\geq1/2$, which yields the Brier bound.
\end{proof}

This rules out distribution-free calibration without assumptions or labeled
calibration.  It does not prove that logits are insufficient within one fixed
process, and the same adversarial construction applies to any fixed hybrid
observable.  Whether internal states, semantic features, or gap coordinates
help is an empirical question.

\section{Exact and relaxed speculative verification}

Let a draft distribution $q$ propose a token $X$, let $a(x)\in[0,1]$ be its
acceptance probability, and after rejection draw a replacement from $h$.  Set
\[
  r=1-\sum_xq(x)a(x).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Acceptance/residual decomposition]\label{thm:residual}
The final output follows target distribution $p$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-condition}
  q(x)a(x)+r h(x)=p(x)
  \quad\text{for every }x.
\end{equation}
When $r>0$, such an $h$ exists exactly when $q(x)a(x)\leq p(x)$ for every $x$,
and then
\[
  h(x)=\frac{p(x)-q(x)a(x)}{r}.
\]
When $r=0$, exactness holds exactly when $q(x)a(x)=p(x)$ for every $x$,
equivalently $q=p$ with full acceptance on the support of $q$; acceptance at
$q$-null points is immaterial.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:residual-condition} is the law of total probability over
acceptance and rejection.  For $r>0$, solving for $h$ gives the formula.
Nonnegativity is equivalent to $qa\leq p$, and the numerator sums to $r$, so
$h$ is normalized.  If $r=0$, the residual term vanishes, giving the stated
componentwise condition.
\end{proof}

For $q(x)>0$, the standard maximal choice
\[
  a(x)=\min\{1,p(x)/q(x)\}
\]
has accepted mass $\min\{p(x),q(x)\}$.  It maximizes the total acceptance
probability because exactness requires $qa\leq p$ and $a\leq1$ gives
$qa\leq q$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:max-acceptance}
  A_{\max}=\sum_x\min\{p(x),q(x)\}=1-\TV(p,q).
\end{equation}

Conditional on acceptance, when the acceptance probability is nonzero,
\[
 \Pr(X=x\mid\text{accepted})
 =\frac{q(x)a(x)}{\sum_yq(y)a(y)}.
\]
This conditional law need not itself equal $p$; the residual completes the
target distribution.

Now suppose a target-only gate accepts a proposed token of target rank
$r\leq k$ when
\[
  \frac{s_1-s_r}{D}\leq\rho.
\]
Corollary~\ref{cor:rank-ratio} gives the transparent identity
\[
  \frac{p_r}{p_1}=e^{s_r-s_1}\geq e^{-\rho D}.
\]
This gate omits the proposal distribution $q$ and generally fails
\eqref{eq:residual-condition}.  A normalized threshold also permits an
arbitrarily small ratio as $D$ grows.  Adding an absolute cap
\[
  s_1-s_r\leq\varepsilon
\]
ensures $p_r/p_1\geq e^{-\varepsilon}$, but still does not make the rule exact.
Lossless uses of the geometry may change draft length or verification effort
while retaining the standard $p/q$ accept-and-correct mechanism.

This distinction separates three objectives: exact sampling preserves $p$;
relaxed verification intentionally changes the distribution and must measure
that change; semantic or judge-based decoding optimizes a different downstream
criterion.  Margin-aware relaxed verification and stronger acceptance
certificates already exist \cite{song2026mars,sharma2026}; the elementary ratio
identity above does not supersede them.

\section{A conditional selective-risk guarantee}

The coordinates do not supply calibration by themselves.  One limited but
exact wrapper can be stated under explicit sampling assumptions.  Let
$(S_i,E_i)_{i=1}^{N}$ be IID score/error pairs from the deployment law, fix a
finite threshold grid $\mathcal{T}$ before observing them, and define
\[
  r_t=\Pr(E=1\mid S\geq t).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Simultaneous binomial risk bounds]
For every threshold meeting a predeclared minimum accepted count, compute a
one-sided Clopper--Pearson upper bound at level
$\alpha/|\mathcal{T}|$ \cite{clopper1934}.  With probability at least
$1-\alpha$, every reported bound simultaneously covers its corresponding
$r_t$.  On that event, any threshold selected because its bound is at most a
target risk $\rho$ satisfies $r_t\leq\rho$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on the number accepted at one fixed threshold, accepted errors
are Bernoulli with parameter $r_t$.  The exact one-sided bound fails with
probability at most $\alpha/|\mathcal{T}|$.  A union bound over the predeclared
grid gives simultaneous coverage; selection among simultaneously valid bounds
preserves the statement.
\end{proof}

The assumptions are substantive.  Calibration-set reuse, adaptive threshold
search outside the fixed grid, dependence, and arbitrary distribution shift
are not covered.  More specialized conformal reasoning methods make different
assumptions and targets \cite{zhou2026orca}; saying only ``wrap the score with
conformal prediction'' is not a complete method specification.

\section{Empirical protocol and falsifiable hypotheses}

Although no LLM experiment is reported, the theory determines what a valid
future test must control.  At minimum, a preregistered benchmark should include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item a same-capacity model on relative sorted top-$k$ logits, plus an
        unrestricted sorted-logit offset control;
  \item $u$-only, $D$-only, $(D,u)$, token-identity, and tail-log-mass ablations;
  \item maximum probability, entropy, raw margin, conditional top-$k$ entropy,
        semantic and hidden-state methods, and hybrid baselines;
  \item several predeclared values of $k$, temperature rescaling, and
        within-domain, cross-domain, cross-model, and prompt shifts;
  \item question- or sequence-disjoint train, calibration, and untouched test
        splits; and
  \item AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, negative log-likelihood, full risk--coverage
        curves, AURC, paired uncertainty intervals, latency, and memory.
\end{itemize}

For approximate speculative decoding, exact and relaxed methods must appear in
separate tables.  Throughput must be compared at matched downstream quality or
declared divergence, not at an unmatched acceptance rate.

The only empirical hypotheses retained are:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2em,style=nextline,itemsep=3pt]
  \item[H1.] Normalized gap coordinates may provide a useful finite-sample
  inductive bias in regimes where local sorted-logit shape matters, but should
  not outperform an information-matched comparator merely by containing more
  information.
  \item[H2.] Adding identities, tails, semantic features, or internal probes
  may improve shift robustness; Proposition~\ref{prop:no-free-lunch} does not
  prove that any such hybrid will succeed.
  \item[H3.] Gap diagnostics may help allocate exact verification effort or
  draft length.  Any distribution-changing margin rule must be evaluated
  against MARS and exact speculative sampling.
\end{description}

These hypotheses are falsifiable and are not promoted to conclusions in this
paper.

\section{Limitations and consequences}

\paragraph{Prior-art limitation.}
Outer-difference dependence is classical \cite{prichett1981}.  The contribution
is the explicit injective code and its graph-theoretic consequences, not the
original discovery of the coordinate idea.

\paragraph{Finite versus universal evidence.}
The exhaustive certificate covers exactly its stated grid.  Decimal basin
shares are finite fixed-width results and change if the input law or
leading-zero policy changes.  The universal claims depend on the displayed
proofs; only a subset is currently formalized in Lean.

\paragraph{Representation loss.}
Sorting drops token identity, top-$k$ truncation drops tail detail, and
normalization drops scale unless $D$ is retained.  Even $(D,u)$ drops the
common offset, which can matter outside shift-invariant observation models.
The representation becomes ill-conditioned near flat spectra and changes
with $k$.

\paragraph{No empirical LLM result.}
There are no real-model logits, correctness labels, distribution shifts,
latency measurements, or human evaluations in this work.  No state-of-the-art,
production-safety, speedup, or educational-impact claim is supported.

\paragraph{Operational consequences.}
For finite-dynamics research, the reduction replaces exponential raw
enumeration with an exact polynomial fixed-base graph and preserves raw basin
weights.  For security, the same collapse is a warning against using the map
as a hash or entropy mechanism.  For LLM decision systems, false acceptance
can expose users to incorrect answers while false rejection adds cost and
withholds useful answers; both must be measured at a declared risk--coverage
point.  For speculative decoding, a target-only margin can be useful as a
diagnostic but cannot silently be relabeled exact sampling.

\section{Conclusion}

The durable mathematical object behind 6174 is an ordered-gap law.  Its
explicit linear reconstruction is superincreasing, which yields an exact
one-step image count and a conjugacy between the post-first-step dynamics and
a polynomial-size reduced graph at fixed base.  Fiber weights then recover the
complete functional graph, raw basins, depth histograms, and entropy evolution.
Exact decimal computations demonstrate why cycles, not only fixed points, are
essential to the real dynamics.

Normalized adjacent gaps also provide a precise coordinate system for sorted
top-$k$ logits.  Their value is representational and diagnostic: they separate
spread from relative shape and expose exact rank ratios.  Their limitations
are equally exact: they add no information relative to relative sorted logits,
are singular at flat spectra, omit tail detail, and cannot universally
calibrate correctness.  Likewise, target-only margin acceptance is not a
substitute for the accept-and-residual condition required to preserve a target
distribution.  This combination of positive structure and negative limits is
the paper's intended practical contribution.
